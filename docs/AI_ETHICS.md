# AI & Technology Ethics — The Interface Grove

Pilot 01, published November 9, 2026. Content and question-bank revision 1.

Start: https://a-feraci.github.io/lumen-and-loam/#lesson/ai-ethics-01

## First lesson
**Should we let the system decide?** asks when a useful prediction justifies delegating decisions that affect people. About 40 minutes including a reflection; no prerequisite is enforced.

The original, explicitly fictional Wayfinder case follows evening shuttle allocation in an off-world forest settlement. The four evidence lenses show riders, model performance, dispatch operations, and data collection. Three provisional decisions reveal tradeoffs rather than producing a moral score. The memo button carries the selected recommendation into an editable notebook draft; pressing Save preserves it. Evidence exploration itself is temporary.

Six learning goals cover prediction versus justification, affected people, autonomy/privacy, averages/fairness, accountability/appeal, and a revisable recommendation. Six teaching sections, examples, definitions, recap, cross-subject links, and primary sources use the same structure as Philosophy. The 24-question bank samples three questions per goal (18 total), independently shuffles questions and choices, and passes at 15/18. It tests concepts and reasoning, not whether the learner chooses a preferred verdict. Sources use the named NIST AI RMF 1.0 (2023) edition and OECD principles; the lesson dates its review and acknowledges NIST's revision in progress.

## Why two subjects
Philosophy introduces argument tools. AI ethics applies them to concrete objectives, data choices, institutions, and affected people. Keep shared navigation and learning records consistent while allowing different exercises. Do not rewrite the Philosophy pilot to make the two lessons identical.

Try reading and reflecting in each class, leaving a partially answered assessment in one while opening the other, then returning. Compare clarity, usefulness, pace, repetition, and activity variety. Both lessons have **Capture my class feedback**: a private notebook draft with prompts for what helped, what repeated, how the subjects felt different, and one next idea. Nothing is sent automatically.

## Seeds, not promises
The three “Beyond this clearing” cards preview possible lessons on privacy/consent, fairness under a budget, and companions/trust/persuasion. They are explicitly proposed and have no fake lesson links. The Interface Grove and lantern case add atmosphere; they do not establish new creature canon. Future visuals, lore, and activities can grow from pilot feedback.
