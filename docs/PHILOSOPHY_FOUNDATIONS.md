# Philosophy Foundations / The Question Grove

A beginner path for learning to examine reasons, values, and assumptions. Lessons should be teachable in conversation, readable on the website, and useful for independent study.

## Published
**01 · What makes a good reason?** Six goals: inquiry/clarification; branches; arguments/assumptions; validity/soundness/induction; objections; facts and values. Approx. 35 minutes including a short reflection. A 24-question bank supplies 18 balanced questions per assessment, 80% to pass. The grade concerns the assessment only, not automatic evaluation of the reflection.

## Proposed sequence — not published
02 · Claims, concepts, and definitions: ambiguity, necessary/sufficient conditions, scope.
03 · Arguments that hold: valid forms, plausible support, fallacies, and steelmanning.
04 · What do we know?: belief, evidence, justification, uncertainty, disagreement.
05 · What matters?: values, consequences, duties, care, and the limits of introductory labels.
06 · Philosophy across traditions: contextualized readings, shared questions, and meaningful differences. Diverse traditions should also appear throughout the course rather than being isolated to one final lesson.

Possible later branches: identity and mind; beauty and interpretation; justice and institutions; technology and human flourishing. AI & Technology Ethics will reuse the argument-mapping skills from Lesson 01.
